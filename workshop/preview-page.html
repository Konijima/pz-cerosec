The desktop computers of Knox County still work. This one runs Unix, and runs the building.

CeroSec puts a small 1993 Unix on Knox County's desktop computers. Switch one on: a green 60x20 terminal, a BIOS line, a login prompt, files, permissions, accounts, a shell with pipes and cron, three ways off the machine, and the building itself under /dev, by hand, by schedule or by sensor.

The disk belongs to the computer, not to you: walk away and come back to the same session. No dependencies, Build 42, safe on a save you are playing.

Everything is documented in the wiki, the real place to learn CeroSec: its command examples are replayed against the mod's own engine, not typed from memory.

  • Players: Your First Computer, Your First Session, Recipes, Troubleshooting.
  • Modders and admins: Architecture, Contributing, Sandbox Options, Multiplayer and Servers.

  • A real terminal. Sixty columns by twenty, a BIOS, a login prompt and a shell; two survivors at one computer type on one glass.
  • Files, permissions, accounts. Owner, group and other, octal and symbolic modes, sudo over a real /etc/sudoers.
  • A real shell. sh's grammar, pipes, redirects, background jobs, cron and a screen editor: more than eighty commands.
  • The building, under /dev. A character device for every door, lock, window, curtain or appliance, once one of nine screw-on modules is on it: dev curtain0 open.
  • Floppy disks. A 3.5 inch disk with its own filesystem: newfs, mount, write on the label, carry it across town.
  • A manual you have to find. Three volumes printed in 1993, and your survivor works on while you read.
[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h312-automation.png

A fixture with a module on it is a file, and anything that can write a file can work it: a word at the prompt, a script, a crontab line.

  • A door that shuts itself, five seconds after somebody walked through.
  • Curtains at dawn and dusk, or drawn across the building in one word.
  • The generator watched, with a letter to root when the tank gets low, once, not sixty times an hour.
  • An alarm that names the window somebody opened, on every screen in the building, and flashes the lights.

These are four of the six programs on a printed floppy, CeroSec HOME 1.0; the other two run the TV and wake the house. A module goes on the fixture first: a door, a window or a curtain from inside and standing open, the rest where it stands. The four that move something want a Small Motor, and Knox County never sold one, so you take a screwdriver to a hair dryer.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h312-getting-started.png
  • Right-click a desktop computer, Turn on computer (it needs power), then Use computer.
  • At login: most machines are somebody's: the password is on a paper in a drawer or a dead man's pocket, and there is no password recovery. A machine nobody owned takes admin and an empty password.
  • help lists the commands, dev the fixtures, empty until a module is on one.
  • Walk away without typing exit and the session is still there when you come back; exit logs you out.

Every disk lives on the server; a client asks, never decides, and is never told a password. A script is stepped, never run to the end, so a while true costs its own share, and nothing a player types reaches Lua.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h312-faithful.png

Where a real /bin/sh, cron or Hayes modem answers a certain way, this one answers the same, refusals included. A few things have no 1993 model and are kept anyway; the in-game Volume 1 names each one.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h312-faq.png

Can I add it to a save I am already playing? Yes: its data stays under its own key, and it replaces no vanilla behaviour.

Will it slow the game down? No: a script is stepped, never run to the end, and a dark machine costs nothing.

Does it conflict with other mods? It replaces no vanilla Lua or script. Works beside Computer Mod: a dark desktop offers both menus, and whichever mod switches it on owns it until it is off.

Build 41? No, and none planned: Build 42 only, 42.20 and up.
