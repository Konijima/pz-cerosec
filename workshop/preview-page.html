CeroSec - Unix Terminal and Home Automation

Made by Konijima · Project Zomboid · Build 42

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/banner.png

The desktop computers of Knox County still work. This one runs Unix, and runs the building.

CeroSec puts a small 1993 Unix on the vanilla desktop computers of Knox County. Switch one on, sit down at it, and a green 60x20 terminal opens on a BIOS line and a login prompt. Behind it: files, permissions, accounts, a shell with pipes and cron, three ways off the machine, and the building itself under /dev -- doors, lights, curtains, windows, the oven, the generator, the television -- worked by hand, on a schedule or off a sensor.

The disk belongs to the computer, not to you: walk away and come back to the same session. No dependencies, Build 42, safe on a save you are playing.

  • A real terminal. Sixty columns by twenty, a BIOS, a login prompt and a shell; two survivors at one computer type on one glass.
  • Files, permissions, accounts. Owner, group and other, octal and symbolic modes, links, sudo over a real /etc/sudoers.
  • The prompt is the script language. sh's grammar with pipes, redirects and background jobs, plus cron and a screen editor.
  • The building, under /dev. A character device for every door, lock, window, light, curtain, oven, washer, generator and set it can reach, once one of nine screw-on modules is on the fixture: dev curtain0 open.
  • Floppy disks. A 3.5 inch disk with its own filesystem: newfs, mount, write on the label, carry it across town.
  • A manual you have to find. Three volumes printed in 1993, and your survivor works on while you read.

A fixture with a module on it is a file, and anything that can write a file can work it: a word at the prompt, a script, a crontab line.

  • A door that shuts itself, five seconds after somebody walked through.
  • Curtains at dawn and dusk, or drawn across the building in one word.
  • The television on for Life and Living, tuned for the programme and off at the end.
  • The generator watched, with a letter to root when the tank gets low -- once, not sixty times an hour.
  • An alarm that names the window somebody opened, on every screen in the building, and flashes the lights.
  • A morning: the radio and every light on at half past six.

The six are programs on a printed floppy, CeroSec HOME 1.0, found in the same drawers as the other disks. Copy them over, put them in a crontab:

$ echo close > /dev/door0
$ crontab -e
0 7 * * * sh /usr/local/bin/curtains.sh auto
0 20 * * * sh /usr/local/bin/curtains.sh auto

What it costs. A module goes on the fixture first, from inside, door open, and the four that move something want a Small Motor -- Knox County never sold one, so you take a screwdriver to a hair dryer. A window operator also throws the catch on its way past: in an alarmed house, a window opening at six in the morning rings it.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h-getting-started.png
  • Right-click a desktop computer, Turn on computer (it needs power), then again for Use computer: your survivor sits down.
  • At login: a machine nobody set up takes admin and an empty password. On somebody's machine the accounts are his, and the password is on a paper in a drawer or a dead man's pocket.
  • help lists the commands, dev the fixtures -- empty until a module is on one: right-click the door, CeroSec hardware.
  • Type exit and walk away. The screen keeps running, exactly as you left it.

  • Somebody's machine, not a bare one. A computer nobody has switched on yet comes up as its owner's: hostname, accounts, files, log.
  • Ten kinds of premises, from a house and a shop to a bank, a clinic, a radio station, a military post and CeroSec's own bench.
  • Their crontab lines still run. A shop's lights go off at nine, a vault pulls to at six.
  • The password is written down, because everybody wrote it down. A sticky note in a desk, or in a dead employee's pocket.
  • Disks, and the phone book. Seventeen floppies in a hundred are not blank, and vanilla's Phonebook is the county directory.

  • Coax, for the premises. Every computer of a premises shares a segment and an address it did not choose: arp, ruptime, rlogin, rsh, rcp.
  • The telephone, for the county. One line per premises, one call at a time, a seven-digit number the firmware announces at power-on.
  • Packet radio, for after the power. A two-way radio becomes the machine's TNC and a callsign makes it a station: the only link that outlives the grid.

Every disk lives on the server and every command runs there. A client asks; it never decides, and is never told a password.

The budgets are why it is safe on a public server. A script is stepped, never run to the end, so a while true costs that machine its own share and nothing else, and nothing a player types reaches Lua.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h-faithful.png

Where a real /bin/sh, cron, rlogin or Hayes modem would answer a certain way, this machine answers the same, refusals included.

Six things have no 1993 model and are kept anyway; the in-game Volume 1 names them.

[img] from a host this preview does not resolve: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Konijima/pz-cerosec-media/main/img/h-faq.png

Can I add it to a save I am already playing? Yes, and removing it leaves the computers as they were.

Will it slow the game down? No: a script is stepped, never run to the end, and a dark machine costs nothing.

Does it conflict with other mods? Nothing known: it overrides no vanilla file.

Build 41? No, and there will not be one: Build 42 only, 42.20 and up.
