CeroSec - Unix Network Terminal

Made by Konijima · Project Zomboid · Build 42

The desktop computers of Knox County still work. This one runs Unix.

CeroSec puts a small 1993 Unix on the vanilla desktop computers already in the world. Right-click one to switch it on, right-click again to sit down, and a green 60x20 terminal opens on a BIOS line and a login prompt. Behind it: files, permissions, accounts, a shell with pipes and cron, the building's doors and lights under /dev, and three ways off the machine.

The disk belongs to the computer, not to you, so you walk away and come back to the same session. No dependencies, Build 42, safe to add to a save you are already playing.

SHOT 01 -- workshop/shots/01-boot-login.png, the hero shot
  • A real terminal. Sixty columns by twenty, a BIOS, a login prompt and a shell, and two survivors at one computer type on one glass.
  • Files, permissions, accounts. Owner, group and other, octal and symbolic modes, links, and sudo over a real /etc/sudoers.
  • The prompt is the script language. sh's grammar with pipes, redirects and background jobs, plus cron and a screen editor.
  • The building, under /dev. A character device for every door, switch, lock and window the machine can reach: dev light0 off, dev door1 open.
  • Hardware you have to fit. A contact, a relay, a strike or a door operator goes on the fixture itself, with a screwdriver and Electricity.
  • Floppy disks. A 3.5 inch disk with a filesystem of its own: newfs, mount, write on the label, carry it across town.
  • A manual you have to find. Three volumes printed in 1993, and your survivor works on while you read.

SHOT 02 -- workshop/shots/02-shell-session.png
  • Right-click a desktop computer, Turn on computer. It needs power.
  • Right-click again, Use computer. Your survivor walks over and sits down.
  • At login: try admin and press Enter at the password: a bare machine has admin and root, both open.
  • help lists the commands, dev the doors and lights. That list is empty until a module is on the fixture: right-click the door, CeroSec hardware.
  • Type exit and walk away. The screen keeps running, exactly as you left it.

  • Somebody's machine, not a bare one. A computer nobody has switched on yet comes up with its owner's hostname, accounts, files and log; one already switched on is never touched.
  • Ten kinds of premises. A house, an office, a dispatch room, a bank, a shop, a school, a clinic, a radio station, a military post and CeroSec's own bench.
  • Their crontab lines still run. A shop's lights go off at nine, a vault pulls to at six.
  • The password is written down, because everybody wrote it down. A sticky note in a desk of that premises, or a dead employee with his login in his pocket, and the item's name is the password.
  • Disks, and the phone book. Seventeen floppies in a hundred are not blank, and vanilla's Phonebook is the Knox County directory.

  • Coax, for the premises. Every computer of one premises shares a segment and an address it did not choose, with arp, ruptime, rlogin, rsh and rcp. A mall is thirty shops on thirty segments.
  • The telephone, for the county. One line per premises, one call at a time, a seven-digit number the firmware announces at power-on and nowhere else.
  • Packet radio, for after the power. A two-way radio becomes the machine's TNC and a callsign in /etc/callsign makes it a station. The only link that outlives the grid.

Every disk lives on the server and every command runs there. A client asks; it never decides, and no client is told a password.

The budgets are why it is safe on a public server. A script is stepped, never run to the end, so a while true costs that machine its own share and nothing else. A disk is 64 KB and the fifth remote session is refused. Nothing a player types is ever handed to Lua.

Where a real /bin/sh, cron, rlogin or Hayes modem would answer a certain way, this machine answers the same way, refusals included.

Six things have no 1993 model and are kept anyway, and rather than pass them off quietly they are named in the in-game Volume 1.

Does it need any other mod? No: no dependencies, no bundled libraries, vanilla Lua only.

Can I add it to a save I am already playing? Yes, and removing it leaves the computers as the furniture they were.

Will it slow the game down? No: a script is stepped rather than run to the end, and the ceilings above are the whole of what one computer can cost.

Does it work in multiplayer? Yes, built that way rather than adapted, with dedicated-server testing next on the list.

Does it conflict with other mods? Nothing known, because it overrides no vanilla file.

Build 41? No, and there will not be one: Build 42 only, on 42.20 and up.
