CeroSec - Unix Network Terminal

Made by Konijima · Project Zomboid · Build 42

The desktop computers of Knox County still work. This one runs Unix.

CeroSec puts a small 1993 Unix on the vanilla desktop computers already standing in the world. Right-click one to switch it on, right-click again to sit down at it, and a green 60x20 terminal opens on a BIOS line and a login prompt. Behind it: files, permissions, accounts, a shell with pipes and cron, the building's own doors and lights under /dev, and three ways off the machine.

The disk belongs to the computer, not to you. Walk away and come back to the same session, still logged in.

No dependencies. Build 42. Safe to add to a save you are already playing.

SHOT 01 -- workshop/shots/01-boot-login.png, the hero shot
  • A real terminal. Sixty columns by twenty, a BIOS, a login prompt, a shell. Two survivors at one computer type on the same glass.
  • Files, permissions, accounts. Owner, group and other, octal and symbolic modes, symbolic links, and su and sudo with a real /etc/sudoers behind them.
  • The prompt is the script language. sh's own grammar: if, while, for, variables, $( ), pipes, redirects, background jobs. cron runs a crontab while nobody is watching; edit turns the screen into an editor.
  • The building, under /dev. A character device for every door, switch, lock and window the machine can reach: dev light0 off, dev door1 open. A vanilla Motion Sensor becomes a sensorN a script can poll.
  • Hardware you have to fit. Nothing is a device because of what it is. A contact, a relay, an electric strike or a door operator goes on the fixture itself, with a screwdriver and some Electricity. Find them in an electrician's van, or read the CeroSec Field Wiring Guide.
  • Floppy disks. A 3.5 inch disk with a filesystem of its own: newfs, mount, copy your notes on, write on the label, carry it across town.
  • A manual you have to find. Three volumes, printed in 1993, spawning where a book about a computer would have been shelved. Your survivor goes on working while you read.

  • Right-click a desktop computer, Turn on computer. It needs power in the room.
  • Right-click again, Use computer. Your survivor walks over and sits down.
  • At login: try admin and press Enter at the password. A bare machine has admin and root, both open; somebody's machine has his accounts, and some are locked.
  • help lists the commands, dev the doors, lights and locks. That list is empty until a module is on the fixture: right-click the door itself and take CeroSec hardware.
  • Type exit, or close the window, and walk away. The screen keeps running and is exactly as you left it.

Knox County had computers in it, and people were using them, so a machine nobody has switched on yet comes up as somebody's. A sandbox option, on by default; a machine already switched on is never touched.

  • Ten kinds of premises. A house, an office, a dispatch room, a bank, a shop, a school, a clinic, a radio station, a military post and CeroSec's own bench, each with its own hostname, accounts, files and log.
  • Their crontab lines still run. A shop's lights go off at nine, a bank's vault pulls itself to at six.
  • The password is written down, because everybody wrote it down. A sticky note in a desk of that premises, or a dead employee with his login in his pocket. The name of the item in your inventory is the password, and it is right before you switch the machine on.
  • Disks with something on them. Seventeen floppies in a hundred are not blank: utilities, a BBS list, games, somebody's backup of his home directory on the 8th of July.
  • The phone book is a phone book. Vanilla's own Phonebook item is the Knox County directory, and the copy remembers which town it came from.

  • Coax, for the premises. Every computer of one premises shares a segment and an address that comes out of where it stands. A premises is not a building: a house is one, a mall is thirty shops on thirty segments. arp, ruptime, rlogin, rsh, rcp, and trust files so you stop typing the password.
  • The telephone, for the county. One line per premises, one call at a time, a seven-digit number the firmware announces at power-on and nowhere else. cu dials it at 2400 baud.
  • Packet radio, for after the power. A two-way radio the machine can reach becomes its TNC, and a callsign in /etc/callsign makes it a station. cu -l /dev/radio0 opens the box's own prompt with the TNC-2's command set. The only link that outlives the county's power.

Every disk lives on the server and every command runs there. A client asks; it never decides, and no client is ever told a password.

The budgets are why it is safe on a public server. A script is stepped, never looped to the end, so a while true somebody left running costs that machine its own share and nothing else. A disk is 64 KB, a file 4096 bytes, a machine 512 nodes; four jobs is four jobs and the fifth remote session is refused. Nothing a player types is ever handed to Lua. Admins get a debug window and a diagnostics floppy.

Where a real /bin/sh, cron, rlogin or Hayes modem would answer a certain way, this machine answers the same way, refusals included. The commands wear the names 1993 gave them: useradd, never Debian's adduser.

Six things have no 1993 model and are kept anyway. Rather than pass them off quietly they are printed in the in-game Volume 1, on a page called What is not Unix here: help, dev, mkpasswd, edit, sudo, and jobs that belong to the machine rather than to the shell.

Does it need any other mod? No. No dependencies, no bundled libraries, vanilla Lua only.

Can I add it to a save I am already playing? Yes. The computers were always there; this gives them a disk the first time you switch one on. Removing it leaves them as the furniture they were.

Will it slow the game down? It is budgeted rather than trusted: the ceilings above are the whole of what one computer can cost. Nor can an update break what you built, because every release photographs the shape it writes to the save and the next has to walk it forward.

Does it work in multiplayer? Yes, and it was built that way rather than adapted. Dedicated-server testing is next on the list, so if you run one, say what you find in the comments.

Does it conflict with other mods? Nothing known: it adds its own items and menu entries and overrides no vanilla file. A mod that replaces the desktop computer object itself would collide.

Build 41? No, and there will not be one. Build 42 only, on 42.20 and up.
